The plan

Harry's daily plan

Histamine is a chemical the body releases when it feels under attack. It drives itch, redness and swelling in the skin. This plan lowers histamine from every direction at once: less coming in from food, less being released, and more support for the skin and gut to repair.

1

Low histamine food

Use the food lists below for four weeks. Fresh food, cooked and eaten fresh.

2

Zyrtec with dinner

Every night with dinner, even on good skin days. It can make Harry a little sleepy, which helps him sleep through the night instead of scratching.

3

Supplements

Albizia Complex, Ultra Flora Kids Care and zinc. The full timetable is just below. A calmer gut helps the body handle histamine.

4

Red light mask

Red light for 20 minutes every night, plus blue light for 15 minutes every second night. The research is further down this page.

5

Quick, cool showers

Keep showers short and cool. No hot baths or long hot showers while the skin settles, as heat can set off the itch.

How it all works together

Between the supplements, the added antihistamine and fewer high histamine foods, Harry's body has much less histamine to deal with. At the same time, his gut gets the building blocks it needs to take up nutrients better and become less sensitive.

About Ultra Flora Kids Care

Made by Metagenics. It has three types of friendly gut bacteria: Bifidobacterium lactis, Lactobacillus acidophilus and Lactobacillus rhamnosus. It also has fructooligosaccharides (FOS), a natural plant fibre that feeds those good bacteria. Keep it in the fridge.

Red and infrared light

What the research says

Red and near-infrared light therapy is also called photobiomodulation. The light is absorbed by the mitochondria, the parts of each cell that make energy. Here is what the studies show so far, in plain words.

It calms inflammation

A review from Harvard's Wellman Center for Photomedicine found that one of the most reliable effects of red and near-infrared light is an overall reduction in inflammation. It lowers inflammatory signals in cells that are already inflamed and boosts the body's own antioxidant defences.

Hamblin, AIMS Biophysics, 2017 [1]

It supports skin repair

A review of red and near-infrared light for slow-healing skin wounds found it can help repair and reduce inflammation and pain. Near-infrared (800 to 830nm) was the most studied, followed by red (630 to 680nm).

Yadav and Gupta, Photodermatology, Photoimmunology and Photomedicine, 2017 [2]

It eased red, sensitive skin

In a Seoul National University study, 28 people with sensitive, reactive skin had low-level light therapy. Every person improved, redness dropped measurably, and no side effects were reported. It took about 10 sessions on average.

Choi and colleagues, Lasers in Medical Science, 2013 [3]

Eczema itch and rash

In a Japanese study of 112 people with eczema treated with low-level laser light, itch eased in 71% and the rash improved in 62%, with no side effects. It was an early study without a comparison group.

Morita and colleagues, Keio Journal of Medicine, 1993 [4]

Healing beyond the skin

The same Harvard review reports reduced inflammation in joints, lungs, wounds, the brain and abdominal fat, mostly in animal studies. Australian researchers at the University of Newcastle and the University of Sydney describe light on one part of the body producing benefits in distant tissues, supported by many animal experiments and early human data.

Hamblin 2017 [1]; Johnstone and Gordon, International Review of Neurobiology, 2026 [5]

Why blue light too

A University of Manchester review found blue light can calm inflammation and kill some bacteria. Skin conditions like eczema often have too much of certain bacteria on the skin, and blue light may help bring that balance back. In a 2025 study in mice, red and blue light together eased eczema.

Serrage and colleagues, Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology, 2024 [17]; Zhang and colleagues, Photodermatology, Photoimmunology and Photomedicine, 2025 [18]

The honest picture

Research on light therapy for eczema is still small and early, and a 2023 review of LED studies found eczema results varied a lot between studies [6]. That's why we use the mask as gentle daily support alongside the food changes, Zyrtec, supplements and cool showers, and keep your GP in the loop.

More light is not better. Light works best at the right dose, and too much can blunt the effect [1], so stick to the recommended time.

More research

Red light and healing

Red and near-infrared light has been studied for decades, in hospitals and research centres around the world. Here are some of the strongest studies on how it helps the body heal, on the skin and deeper inside.

Healing the skin

18 trials · 670 wounds

Wounds healed faster, with less pain

A 2024 review by UK hospital doctors pooled 18 controlled trials. Wounds treated with low-level light shrank more, closed fully more often, and were less painful than untreated wounds.

Taha and colleagues, Cureus, 2024 [7]

7 trials · 194 people

Slow-healing diabetic ulcers

Diabetic foot ulcers are some of the hardest wounds to heal. Light therapy made them smaller and made full healing far more likely, with no side effects reported.

Li and colleagues, Diabetes Research and Clinical Practice, 2018 [8]

99 trials · 7,356 people

Top ranked for shrinking ulcers

A 2025 review compared 12 different treatments for diabetic foot ulcers. Low-level light therapy ranked first of all 12 for reducing the size of the ulcer.

Hu and colleagues, PeerJ, 2025 [9]

136 people · controlled trial

Smoother skin and more collagen

Red light twice a week for 30 sessions improved how the skin felt and looked, made it smoother, and increased collagen measured by ultrasound, compared with people who weren't treated.

Wunsch and Matuschka, Photomedicine and Laser Surgery, 2014 [10]

7 studies · 297 people

Scars, including itchy scars

Red light improved burn scars and softened surgical scars. Infrared light reduced scar thickness, pain and itch. Both were well tolerated.

Gaumond and colleagues, Lasers in Medical Science, 2026 [11]

9 people · early study

Stubborn, inflamed psoriasis

People whose psoriasis hadn't responded to usual treatments had red (633nm) and infrared (830nm) LED light, very close to the mask's two settings. Their skin cleared by 60 to 100%.

Ablon, Photomedicine and Laser Surgery, 2010 [12]

Healing deeper in the body

International guideline

Recommended in cancer care

The international cancer supportive care guidelines (MASCC/ISOO) recommend light therapy to prevent painful inflamed mouth sores during some cancer treatments. It is one of the few places light therapy is in official guidelines.

Zadik and colleagues, Supportive Care in Cancer, 2019 [13]

22 trials · 1,063 people

Less knee pain from arthritis

Compared with a fake (placebo) light, real light therapy reduced pain and disability in knee arthritis. Relief kept building for 2 to 4 weeks after treatment ended, with no side effects reported.

Stausholm and colleagues, BMJ Open, 2019 [14]

16 trials · 820 people

Neck pain relief that lasted

This review, led by a Sydney researcher and published in The Lancet, found light therapy reduced neck pain straight away, and for people with long-term neck pain the relief lasted up to 22 weeks.

Chow and colleagues, The Lancet, 2009 [15]

4 trials · 66 people

Faster muscle recovery

After hard exercise, light therapy did better than ice for getting strength back, easing soreness and lowering signs of muscle damage. The studies were small.

Ferlito and colleagues, Lasers in Medical Science, 2022 [16]

A note on these studies. Most used clinic devices aimed at the area being treated, so the mask works on the face and nowhere else. We share them to show how consistently the body responds to this light: calmer inflammation, less pain and faster repair. Many studies are small, and researchers are still working out the best doses.

Every day

Using the mask, and Harry's daily rhythm

Using the mask

  1. Start with a clean, dry face so the light can reach the skin.
  2. Choose the red and infrared setting.
  3. Red and infrared: 20 minutes every night.
  4. Blue: 15 minutes every second night, straight after the red.
  5. Same time each night, like before bed, so it becomes habit.
  6. Harry keeps his eyes closed. Follow the safety guide in the box.
  7. Moisturise afterwards.

Daily rhythm

  • Supplements at breakfast, lunch and dinner (see the timetable)
  • Zyrtec (1 dose) and zinc (25mg) with dinner
  • Short, cool showers, no hot baths
  • Red light 20 minutes every night, blue light 15 minutes every second night
  • Meals from the low histamine list, cooked fresh
  • Plenty of water
  • One line in the notes: food, skin, sleep

When to get help

  • Swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, or trouble breathing or swallowing: call 000.
  • Skin that is weeping, crusting yellow, very painful, or comes with a fever: see your GP the same day, as eczema can become infected.
  • Before starting the mask, check with your GP if Harry takes any medicine that makes skin sensitive to light, or has a condition triggered by light such as epilepsy.

This plan supports the care from your GP and doesn't replace it.
